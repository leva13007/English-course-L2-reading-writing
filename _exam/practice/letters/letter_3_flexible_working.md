## Model Email *(~265 words — formal register, request to named line manager)*

---

**To:** j.harris@company.co.uk
**From:** o.levchenko@company.co.uk
**Subject:** Request for Flexible Working Arrangement
**Date:** 30 June 2026

Dear Mr Harris,

I am writing to formally request a change to my working hours under the company's flexible working policy. I have been a member of the team for over fourteen months and have greatly enjoyed my role as a Customer Service Assistant during this time.

I am currently attending an ESOL Level 2 course at Cardiff and Vale College on Tuesday and Wednesday evenings, which I began in February of this year. The course runs from 5:45pm until 7:45pm, and unfortunately I am finding it increasingly difficult to travel from the Cardiff Bay office to the college in time for the start of each session. As a result, I have been arriving late on several occasions, which is affecting my progress on the course.

I would therefore like to request permission to finish work one hour earlier on Tuesdays and Wednesdays only — that is, at 4:00pm rather than the standard 5:00pm finish. I am fully prepared to make up this time by arriving one hour earlier on those two days, starting at 8:00am instead of 9:00am. This arrangement would have no impact on the total number of hours I work each week.

I believe that completing this qualification will ultimately benefit both myself and the company, as it will improve my ability to communicate clearly with colleagues and customers. I would be very happy to discuss this request at a time that is convenient for you.

I look forward to hearing from you.

Yours sincerely,

Oleh Levchenko
Customer Service Assistant

---

**What makes this email good:**
- Email header replaces address block (To / From / Subject / Date)
- Named recipient → *Yours sincerely*
- Request is **specific** (exact times stated)
- Compromise offered immediately (I will start earlier)
- Ends by offering to discuss — not demanding agreement
