## Note on Register

Many phrasal verbs and idioms are **informal or neutral** in register.  
In formal writing (reports, essays, academic texts), they should be replaced with **formal equivalents**.  
Each item below is presented in two versions to demonstrate this skill.

---

## Part 1 — Phrasal Verbs (12 sentences)

| # | Phrasal Verb | 🗣 Informal | 📝 Formal Equivalent |
|---|-------------|------------|----------------------|
| 1 | **catch up** | I need to catch up with the rest of the class. | It is necessary to reach the same level as the other students. |
| 2 | **come across** | I came across a really useful article yesterday. | A highly relevant article was discovered yesterday while reviewing recent publications. |
| 3 | **figure out** | We need to figure out why sales dropped last year. | It is essential to determine the reasons for the decline in sales last year. |
| 4 | **give way to** | Old industries are giving way to new technology. | Traditional industries are being replaced by emerging technologies. |
| 5 | **go through** | The company went through a really tough period in 2020. | The company experienced a particularly challenging period in 2020. |
| 6 | **hand in** | Don't forget to hand in your report by Friday. | Please ensure that your report is submitted by Friday. |
| 7 | **look forward to** | I'm really looking forward to the conference next week. | I am greatly looking forward to attending the conference next week. |
| 8 | **pick up** | She picked up Spanish really quickly. | She acquired proficiency in Spanish within a remarkably short period. |
| 9 | **pull off** | The team pulled off an impressive result despite the challenges. | The team achieved a remarkable outcome despite the considerable difficulties. |
| 10 | **run out of** | We're running out of time to finish the project. | The team has insufficient time remaining to complete the project within the schedule. |
| 11 | **set up** | They set up a new organisation to help young people. | A new organisation was established with the aim of supporting young people. |
| 12 | **sort out** | We need to sort out the issue with the data before publishing. | The inconsistency in the data must be resolved prior to publication. |

## Part 2 — Idioms (12 sentences)

| # | Idiom | 🗣 Informal | 📝 Formal Equivalent |
|---|-------|------------|----------------------|

## Part 3 — Collocations (12 sentences)

Collocations are already appropriate in formal writing.  
Here the focus is on using them correctly in context — paired with a natural informal alternative to highlight the difference.

| # | Collocation | 🗣 Informal / Natural | 📝 Formal with Collocation |
|---|-------------|----------------------|---------------------------|
| 1 | **make a decision** | We need to decide what to do about this. | We need to make a decision about the most appropriate course of action. |
| 2 | **raise awareness** | We should tell more people about this problem. | We should raise awareness of this problem among the general public. |
| 3 | **carry out research** | They looked into the effects of screen time on children. | They carried out research into the effects of screen time on children. |
| 4 | **meet a deadline** | We have to finish this project on time. | We are required to meet the deadline for this project. |
| 5 | **take action** | Someone needs to do something about this problem. | It is essential that action is taken to address this problem. |
| 6 | **reach a conclusion** | We finally figured out what had gone wrong. | We finally reached a conclusion about what had gone wrong. |
| 7 | **have an impact** | Social media really changes how people think. | Social media has a significant impact on how people think. |
| 8 | **pose a challenge** | This is a really difficult problem for everyone involved. | This continues to pose a significant challenge for all parties involved. |
| 9 | **make progress** | Things are slowly getting better in the region. | Considerable progress is gradually being made in the region. |
| 10 | **put forward a proposal** | They suggested a new way of doing things. | They put forward a proposal for a new approach to the process. |
| 11 | **address an issue** | We need to deal with the problem of rising costs. | We need to address the issue of rising costs within the organisation. |
| 12 | **draw a conclusion** | Based on the data, we can say the trend is positive. | Based on the available evidence, it is possible to draw a conclusion that the trend is positive. |
